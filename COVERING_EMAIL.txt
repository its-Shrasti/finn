WHAT I BUILT

FINN is a mobile app that filters what reaches an investor and tells them who in their own network can help them understand it.

Push. A windowed brief of every filing across a 15-stock portfolio, ranked into "needs you", "worth knowing" and "routine". Over 30 days, 72 filings reduce to 13 cards. Results filings come with Revenue, EBITDA, PBT and PAT QoQ and YoY, plus a line on why the bottom line moved. Price and volume alerts are set as an editable sentence, with per-stock overrides.

Pull. Research that works on any NSE ticker. Its centrepiece is a promise ledger: each guidance item from the last four earnings calls gets a checkable measure, and code scores it against reported numbers. Kaynes kept 0 of 3 scoreable promises; Polycab beat its margin guides and declined to raise them.

Network. It appears inside Push and Pull at the moment it matters, not as a contact list. When HDFC Bank names Anup Bagchi CEO, the card shows your best way in: an IIT Kanpur batchmate who overlapped with him at ICICI Bank for five years while he was Executive Director. It also says plainly that your own IIT Kanpur link is weak, because his graduation year isn't public. For Kaynes' acquisition it names the ex-Kaynes corporate development lead. For Polycab's NCLAT ruling it names an insolvency lawyer, not an ex-Polycab salesperson. When no public profile exists (Dixon's new ED), it says it can't compute a path and points to someone who knows the company instead. In Research, "Who to call" pairs each contact with a question taken from the promise ledger. A small Network tab shows how many material events the network covers: 7 of 8 in the 7 days to 3 Nov, the snapshot date (9 of 10 across the whole snapshot, 15 Oct to 2 Nov) and who the go-to people are for each theme. Ask answers "who knows..." questions on the device.

KEY DECISIONS AND TRADEOFFS

1. No number comes from a model. Every figure, ledger verdict, priority score and network link strength is computed in Python and covered by tests (70).
2. The network is matched in code, and a model may write at most one line. Links come from evidence (same employer with overlapping years, same batch, links the investor entered), discounted for age and shown with their reasons. The "why call them" line is a template in the demo, labelled as one.
3. Real people are never "known". People named in filings link to contacts only through public career overlap, with a source for every fact. Board seats don't count, and years that aren't public are left out.
4. Compliance by default. Contacts who work at the company today are flagged and ranked last, bankers get a "no live mandates" note on fund raises, and reconnect nudges never point at an insider.
5. Cheap by design. Rules classify all 72 captured filings at zero cost, transcripts are keyword-filtered before any model sees them, and a full research run costs under 5 US cents at list prices, re-runs free from a content-hash cache.

ARCHITECTURE

Data pipelines: NSE and BSE filings, yfinance end-of-day prices and screener.in fundamentals and transcripts feed one shared snapshot that is the same for every user. Names in filings are normalised and matched exactly against a register of public career histories; ambiguous names get no link.

Onboarding: the investor starts from their own LinkedIn data export (Connections.csv). An importer turns it into contacts and drops URLs and emails. Nothing is scraped.

Security and privacy: the investor's network lives in a separate per-user overlay, never merged into the shared snapshot. Network questions are answered on the device and never go to a model. The optional model step sees only a pseudonymised contact, the one matching role and the public event, and its output is cached privately. The people in an investor's network haven't consented to being in it. The demo avoids the problem by using fictional people. For a real product, the README sets out a design under India's DPDP Act, 2023. Imported contacts stay pending and are matched only on what they published themselves until they confirm a notice sent from the investor's account. There is one stated purpose. Contacts are erased within 30 days of withdrawal or a request, and a suppression hash stops re-imports. Access control uses row-level security, opt-in sharing per path and an audit log.

LLMs and agents: a cheap model (Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite) does bulk extraction and a strong model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) does the two reasoning steps. There are no autonomous agents; the research is a fixed, auditable map-reduce pipeline. Diligence on people is where I would add the first agent.

Cloud: today the app is one static file on Vercel. In production, a scheduled worker builds the shared snapshot after the close and on each filing, and a small authenticated API serves each user's private overlay from an India-region database.

WHAT IS REAL AND WHAT ISN'T

Real: Q2 FY27 results, 72 filings, 8 call transcripts, and the public careers of executives named in filings, each fact with its source. Simulated: end-of-day prices (labelled SIM), because my build sandbox couldn't reach Yahoo Finance; "snapshot --live" runs the same code on live sources. Synthetic: the entire network, 31 fictional people labelled SYN. The public demo bundles it, which is only acceptable because it is fictional.

LEFT OUT ON PURPOSE
